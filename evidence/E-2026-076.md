# E-2026-076

Short statement
On 6 November 2026 the three approximately 950-foot guyed masts at the Beidweiler longwave transmitter site in Luxembourg were brought down in a controlled demolition, as reported by Radio World on 9 November 2026 (citing RTL and Broadcasting Center Europe, BCE). The site had transmitted Radio Télévision Luxembourg (RTL) longwave AM service on 234 kHz to France using a directional array; operations began at Beidweiler in 1972 (service traced to Radio Luxembourg from Junglinster in 1932), transmitter power was 1,400 kW at start and raised to 2,000 kW in 1974. RTL discontinued 234 kHz transmissions on 2 January 2023, and transmission hardware had since been sold and dismantled. BCE Head of Transmissions Eugène Muller said the masts were brought down in a way intended to protect on-site solar panels; Radio World reported the site will continue to be used for solar generation.

What it changes
- Marks the physical dismantling of a historic high-power European longwave radio transmission site (RTL 234 kHz), a concrete infrastructure milestone after the 2023 end of transmissions.

Verification limits
