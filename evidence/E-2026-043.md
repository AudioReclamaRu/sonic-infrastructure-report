# E-2026-043

Short statement
On 16.11.2026 Slator reported that France's Centre national du cinéma et de l'image animée (CNC), a public administrative body under the authority of France's culture minister, will no longer fund dubbing, subtitling, or audio description projects produced without any human creators. The CNC board adopted the change in a deliberation on October 14, 2026, and the text, published in the Journal officiel on October 31, 2026, limits eligible localization spending to human authors and performers and the technical work around their output. The decision adds a new article to the CNC's general financial-aid regulations: for films, audiovisual works and video games, writing, development and production must "involve at least one author"; animation projects require at least one "graphic author", and CNC-funded original music requires at least one human author responsible for composition. For dubbing, CNC funding can only cover payment to the people who write the text and the voice-actors who perform it, plus the technical work to record, mix and incorporate their performances, which also applies to "post-synchronization", audio description, voice-over and commentary; for subtitling, funding can cover human authors' pay and the technical work to synchronize and incorporate their subtitles. Slator noted the rules stop short of banning AI outright; in a September 8, 2026 answer to a parliamentary question, the culture ministry said the measures were not about prohibiting AI but about promoting human creators. The rule came into effect on November 1, 2026.
