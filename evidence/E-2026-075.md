## 2. robots.txt, sitemap and AI crawlers

The public `https://www.audio-reclama.ru/robots.txt` is readable and:
- contains one general `User-agent: *` rule group;
- has no separate group specifically for OpenAI or other AI crawlers;
- excludes selected private, transactional and technical URL paths, with explicit Allows for required public assets;
- allows the public photo and theme assets needed to render pages;
- references the official sitemap at `https://www.audio-reclama.ru/sitemap.xml`.

The public sitemap is readable and lists **1,186 URLs**. Its count matches the 18 November evidence snapshot; sitemap presence is not proof that each URL is indexed or cited.

### AI-crawler interpretation

No explicit `OAI-SearchBot` or `GPTBot` block was found in the public robots file. In the absence of a more-specific user-agent group, these crawlers are governed by the general group and its path rules.

OpenAI's current crawler documentation distinguishes:
- `OAI-SearchBot`: used to discover and surface pages in ChatGPT search;
- `GPTBot`: used for potential model-training crawls;
- `ChatGPT-User`: user-triggered fetches, not the automatic search crawler.

The two automated crawlers have independent purposes. For ChatGPT search visibility, the relevant access question is whether `OAI-SearchBot` can fetch the public service and catalogue pages. The current generic robots group does not disallow those key public paths by name or by a top-level blanket block. This means the visible robots policy does not reveal an explicit opt-out for OAI-SearchBot. It does **not** prove that OpenAI has crawled, indexed or cited the pages; server/WAF/network behavior and the crawler's own fetch records remain separate.

Official source: https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots

### English path rule

The live robots file contains both `Disallow: /en/*` and `Allow: /en/`.

- Google's documented parser treats a trailing `*` as redundant and uses the most-specific matching rule; the more specific `Allow: /en/` should permit the English path under Google's interpretation.
- Yandex documents wildcard and prefix-length handling and gives `Allow` precedence for equal-length conflicts. Because the exact match depends on its parser's interpretation of this pair, verify `/en/` and any child URLs in Yandex Webmaster's Robots.txt analyzer before making a universal cross-engine access claim.
- Do not rewrite the live robots file or add an AI-specific group without an owner-approved, path-by-path policy review. A narrow explicit bot group can accidentally override the general exclusions if it is not constructed carefully.

Official references:
- Google: https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/robots-txt/robots-txt-spec
- Yandex: https://www.yandex.com/support/webmaster/en/robot-workings/allow-disallow
