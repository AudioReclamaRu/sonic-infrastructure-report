# E-2026-032

Short statement
On 30.10.2026 Radio Ink reported that the Republican National Committee and the
Republican Party of Texas, together with four Republican candidates, sued the FCC
in the US District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Amarillo Division,
over the lowest unit charge for political advertising. Under the law stations must
charge candidates no more than their lowest rate for the same class and amount of
time in the 60 days before a general election and the 45 days before a primary, and
the complaint says party committees never receive that rate even when they run ads
about the same race. The other plaintiffs are Rep. Ronny Jackson of Texas' 13th
Congressional District, Texas Rep. Caroline Fairly and congressional nominees Jace
Yarbrough and Eric Flores; the defendants are the FCC, Chairman Brendan Carr,
Commissioners Anna Gomez and Olivia Trusty, the Justice Department and Attorney
General Todd Blanche, and the Federal Election Commission with Commissioners Shana
Broussard and Dara Lindenbaum. The complaint cites station records: at Amarillo's
KVII-TV a campaign paid $1,050 for a 30-second ad in local evening news while an
ineligible advertiser paid $1,300, and at Houston's KTRK-TV ineligible advertisers
paid $2,600 for 30 seconds in the 11 a.m. news against $400 for a campaign; the rate
is generally three to 13 times cheaper than the ordinary price. A broadcaster that
denies the rate to an eligible buyer faces a penalty of up to $25,000 a day, while
one that grants it to an ineligible buyer could face in-kind contribution liability.
The filing follows FCC Media Bureau guidance of March 30, a June suit by four
Democratic candidates, the Fourth Circuit setting that guidance aside on August 25,
a Supreme Court stay on September 4, a short FCC comment window opened September 15
and a further Supreme Court stay of a follow-up Fourth Circuit order on October 14.
