# E-2026-042 — Republican Party committees challenge broadcast lowest-unit-rate rules

## Short statement

On 28 October 2026, the Republican National Committee, the Republican Party of Texas and four Republican candidates filed a complaint against the Federal Communications Commission and other federal defendants in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Amarillo Division. The Federal Election Commission's public case page lists the matter as active and links the 28 October complaint. A public docket mirror identifies the civil action as 2:2026cv00216.

The complaint challenges the distinction between legally qualified candidates, who are expressly covered by the lowest-unit-charge provision in 47 U.S.C. §315(b)(1)(A), and political party committees. The plaintiffs allege that denying party committees the same rate discriminates between political speakers. They seek extension of the rate to party committees or, in the alternative, a declaration that the candidate-only rule is unconstitutional and unenforceable.

The filing and statutory text are verified facts. The asserted constitutional injury, claimed competitive effects and requested relief are the plaintiffs' allegations and requested remedies, not judicial findings. No final merits ruling is claimed in this record.

## Statutory scope

47 U.S.C. §315(b)(1)(A) states that charges to a legally qualified candidate for use of a broadcasting station in connection with the candidate's campaign may not exceed the station's lowest-unit charge for the same class and amount of time:
- during the 45 days before a primary or primary runoff election; and
- during the 60 days before a general or special election.

The provision names legally qualified candidates. The lawsuit asks whether the candidate/party distinction is constitutional and seeks alternative remedies. This evidence record does not resolve that legal question.
