# E-2026-066 — Public offer structure on comparator studio pages

## Method and boundary

Read-only extraction of publicly accessible commercial pages on 2026-11-18. The figures below are **self-published claims by the named competitor pages**, not independent verification of their actual transaction prices, quality, customer results or availability. They are market examples, not recommended prices for «Аудио-Реклама•ru».

## Captured offer patterns

### SoundFront

Source: https://soundfront.ru/price

- The page presents separate starting prices for a 30-second audio commercial: **4,500 ₽ without music**, **5,600 ₽ with music**, **18,000 ₽ for a vocal version**, and **3,000 ₽ for a spot passport**.
- It states audio-commercial production from one day.
- For phone greetings it lists starting prices and volume tiers; for example, one greeting from 4,500 ₽, two from 7,000 ₽, three from 10,000 ₽, and a two-language greeting from 14,500 ₽.
- It asks the buyer to send a technical brief and name a budget, and says a free demo is available for large orders.
- The page contains separate pricing for video voiceover, localization and other audio services.

### Demyanoff

Source: https://www.demyanoff.ru/stoimost-uslug

- The published price page lists an audio commercial at **4,000 ₽ up to 30 seconds**, an autoanswer greeting with music at **2,500 ₽ for one file up to 30 seconds**, a video voiceover package at **7,000 ₽ up to one minute**, and a jingle at **1,500 ₽ up to 30 seconds**.
- The page also lists prices for scriptwriting, licensed music and translation.
- The workflow explains the order sequence, advance payment, delivery of multiple voice takes and the distinction between a free re-record for an unchanged brief and paid changes to the approved text.
- A separate video page distinguishes a basic voice recording from a more complete package with music and editing: https://www.demyanoff.ru/ozvuchka-video.

### Telegolos

Source: https://telegolos.ru/ceny.html

- The page shows standard IVR/phone-greeting price points including **3,500 ₽** and **5,500 ₽** for a block up to 30 seconds or 60 words, with the higher option listing additional music treatment.
- It lists script help at **2,500 ₽** and treats large multi-level IVR menus as individually quoted.
- It links price information to voice examples, music choices, terms and an order/calculation route.

### Business Sound

Source: https://business-sound.ru/reklamnye-audioroliki

- The page shows an audio-commercial offer from **2,890 ₽** for a 30-second basic tier, **3,890 ₽** for a musical tier and **6,890 ₽** for an optimized package.
- It displays time ranges, a list of included production elements, sample audio and industry-specific examples.
- The same page also presents a conditional free-minute offer and a self-reported catalogue count. Those claims are not independently checked here.

## Repeated buyer-facing patterns

Across these snapshots, the comparator pages make several purchase questions explicit:

1. What does the package include?
2. How is the price calculated, and what is the starting point?
3. What is the expected delivery window?
4. Can the buyer listen to examples or request a demo?
5. Which changes/re-records are included?
6. What rights, documents or final files are delivered?
7. What is the next step to receive a quote?

These patterns are observed in competitor page design. They are not proof that a given price or feature causes higher conversion.

## Restrictions

- Do not copy competitor rates, wording, package names or guarantees.
- Do not infer that competitor rates are comparable without matching duration, languages, voice type, music, editing, rights, usage territory and revision terms.
- Do not present a competitor's page statement as an independently audited fact.
