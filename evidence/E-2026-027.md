# E-2026-027

Short statement
On 23.10.2026 Santa Clara County Superior Court judge Shella Deen overruled Google's
demurrer on all four causes of action in former NPR host David Greene's suit over the
default male AI voice of NotebookLM's Audio Overviews (order filed 23.10.2026). The
ruling does not decide whether the claims are true; at this stage the court takes the
complaint's allegations as true. Greene hosted NPR's Morning Edition for almost 10
years, hosted the Up First podcast and reported on the White House for NPR for four
years. He alleges Google used recordings of his voice to create the default male voice
of Audio Overviews (introduced in September 2024) and then used that voice to market
and sell the product without permission: NotebookLM is sold through paid subscriptions
including Google One AI Premium at $19.99 per month, and the voice was used in a
December 2024 Spotify partnership. The complaint cites listeners who wrote that the
male voice "sounds exactly like" Greene and a forensic voice-recognition firm that
found "relatively high" evidence of a match. Greene filed on 23.01.2026; Google moved
the case to federal court, and a US District Court judge in the Northern District of
California sent it back to state court on 23.07.2026. The four claims are a statutory
right of publicity under California Civil Code section 3344, unfair competition,
common-law misappropriation, and quasi-contract and unjust enrichment.
