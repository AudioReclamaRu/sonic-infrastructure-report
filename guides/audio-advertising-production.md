# Audio advertising production: script, voice, sound design and delivery

**Audience:** brands, agencies, retailers, service businesses and marketing teams  
**Buyer intent:** commission a radio commercial, audio ad, branded spot or multilingual audio campaign asset  
**Status:** English commercial-route draft in the repository; not published on the official website

## Direct answer

A production-ready audio advertisement needs more than a recorded voice. The work usually connects the campaign brief, message and script, voice casting, directed recording, music and sound design, editing, final mix, versions and usage rights. The receiving radio station, streaming platform, in-store network or other channel may have its own duration, loudness, file and metadata requirements. Those requirements should be collected before final delivery.

The public audio-advertising service of «Аудио-Реклама•ru» lists script work, voice casting, sound design, licensed music and transfer of rights among its production elements. The owner confirmed on 19 November 2026 that the studio can organize voice production for any language group worldwide through its international network of collaborators. Each campaign brief defines the target locale, audience, voice, language adaptation, usage rights, delivery formats and schedule.

## Start with the communication job

Before writing a script, specify:
- the product, service or offer;
- the intended audience and target market;
- the action the listener should take;
- the single most important message;
- mandatory brand, legal, price or offer wording;
- campaign dates and any time-limited details;
- channel, placement, duration and number of versions.

A radio spot, streaming audio ad, in-store announcement and a brand sonic signature may share production techniques but have different creative and technical requirements. Do not treat them as interchangeable deliverables.

## The production workflow

### 1. Brief and concept

Translate the campaign objective into a clear listener-facing message. Identify the audience, desired action, required information and constraints. If the brief is not yet a finished script, agree whether scriptwriting or script adaptation is in scope.

### 2. Script and timing

Write for the ear. Use one clear idea, natural spoken syntax and a call to action that can be understood on first hearing. Estimate duration from the actual spoken text and performance pace; do not assume that a word count always maps to the same number of seconds across languages. If the client prefers to supply or order the script separately, the dedicated script-order route is [Заказ сценария](https://www.audio-reclama.ru/zakaz-scenariya.html); scriptwriting then becomes an explicit scope item in the brief.

For multilingual campaigns, each locale may require a different script length or creative adaptation. Preserve the intended message and mandatory information, not the exact sentence structure of the source language.

### 3. Casting and voice direction

Select a voice that fits the audience, brand character, media channel and message. The brief should describe practical voice qualities—register, pace, energy, warmth, authority and pronunciation requirements—rather than rely only on vague labels.

For a campaign spanning multiple languages, organize a coherent voice approach for each locale. The international collaborator network allows the studio to arrange production for any language group worldwide; exact casting and language review are determined by the project brief.

### 4. Recording, music and sound design

Record the approved script under direction. Add music and sound elements that support the message without obscuring the spoken content. Confirm the license and permitted uses for every music or third-party sound asset.

The official service page lists licensed music, sound design, casting and rights transfer as published production elements. The precise items supplied for a project are those confirmed in the quotation and contract.

### 5. Edit and mix for the destination

Check intelligibility, timing, edits, transitions, music-to-voice balance, distortion, clipping, noise and the ending/CTA. Prepare the final mix against the destination's actual technical requirements.

Do not apply one universal loudness number to every delivery. Broadcast television, radio, streaming, social platforms, in-store playback and client systems may use different specifications. For broadcast work, the applicable broadcaster or distributor specification takes precedence. EBU R 128 is one authoritative reference for broadcast programme loudness, but it is not a universal delivery target for every audio advertisement or platform.

### 6. Versions, approval and rights

Maintain a version register for language, duration, offer/date, voice, mix and delivery file. Ensure the approved final copy matches the final recording. Confirm where, for how long and in which territories the client may use the recording, voice performance and music.

## What the buyer receives

The exact package is project-specific, but a well-defined delivery commonly identifies:
- approved script and version;
- final master audio file(s);
- required short/long or market variants;
- language and locale for each version;
- technical format and channel configuration;
- music/third-party asset licensing information as agreed;
- usage-rights scope and final approval record.

Do not assume that media buying, campaign placement, audience targeting or station scheduling is included in audio production. Name those as separate services if they are needed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can the studio produce an audio ad in any language?

The owner confirmed that «Аудио-Реклама•ru» can organize production for any language group worldwide through its international collaborator network. The project brief sets the exact language/locale, voice, script, adaptation, rights and delivery requirements.

### Do I need to provide a finished script?

Not necessarily. The public service page lists script work. The brief should make clear whether the client supplies approved copy, requests creative development or needs adaptation of an existing campaign.

### Can one script simply be translated for every market?

A source message can be shared, but spoken advertising often needs localization or transcreation. Different languages have different pacing, idiom, cultural expectations and legal/offer wording. Each version should be approved for the market where it will run.

### Does the studio buy radio airtime?

The published audio service describes production of the audio asset. Media planning, airtime buying, campaign placement and station scheduling should be treated as separate unless explicitly included in the project agreement.

### Is there one standard loudness level for every audio ad?

No. Requirements depend on the broadcaster, streaming service, in-store system or other destination. Obtain the recipient's delivery specification before final mixing.

## Verification criterion

The advertisement is ready for delivery when the client has approved the script and every localized version, the final master matches the intended timing and destination specification, music and voice rights are documented for the agreed use, and the delivery manifest identifies every file and version.
