# Video voice-over and multilingual localization: how to commission the right production

**Audience:** brands, agencies, product teams, learning teams and video producers  
**Buyer intent:** commission voice-over, dubbing-related work, lip-sync or localized audio for video and presentations  
**Status:** English commercial-route draft in the repository; not published on the official website

## Direct answer

To commission video voice-over or multilingual localization, provide the current video edit, source script or transcript, target language/locale list, timing requirements, creative references and intended distribution. The production route then determines whether the project needs off-screen narration, replacement dialogue, dubbing, lip-sync, or a combination of voice recording and sound design. The localized script and recording must fit the actual picture edit and be reviewed before final delivery.

«Аудио-Реклама•ru» publicly lists voice casting and dubbing actors, ambience and effects, original music, sound design, off-screen voice-over and lip-sync among the elements of its video-voice service. The owner confirmed on 19 November 2026 that the studio can organize production for any language group worldwide through its international collaborator network. The brief defines the precise language/locale, accent, voice, adaptation, picture version, rights and delivery formats.

## First choose the right voice treatment

| Requirement | Production approach | What must be controlled |
|---|---|---|
| Narration over visuals, where original dialogue is absent or remains secondary | Voice-over / off-screen narration | Timing, clarity, script length and placement against the edit |
| Replace original spoken dialogue | Dubbing or dialogue replacement | Character/speaker mapping, timing, pronunciation and performance continuity |
| Match speech to visible mouth movement | Lip-sync-oriented dubbing | Picture lock, timecodes, syllabic timing, phrasing and visual review |
| Produce versions for different markets | Localization / adaptation | Meaning, terminology, cultural context, numbers, legal wording and spoken length |
| Add or rebuild ambience, effects or music | Sound design / audio post-production | Separation of dialogue, music and effects, mix balance and rights |

A project may combine these approaches. Do not call every narration job dubbing, and do not promise frame-accurate lip-sync before the video edit, language and timing constraints are known.

## A reliable international workflow

### 1. Lock the source materials

Identify the exact picture version, frame rate/timecode basis, source dialogue, approved script, reference audio, graphics/text on screen and any required cut-downs. If the picture is still changing, record the edit/version identifier and agree how revisions will be handled.

### 2. Define the language and market matrix

Specify each language and regional variant. Where a video is intended for several markets sharing a language, check whether terminology, accent, references, dates, units or legal notices differ. The studio can organize production for any language group worldwide; the brief determines the appropriate collaborator, voice and review path for each locale.

### 3. Adapt the script for spoken delivery

Check meaning, names, terminology, numbers, on-screen text and legal/product phrases. Adapt line length and timing for the target language while preserving the approved message. For advertising and creative material, transcreation may be more suitable than word-for-word translation.

### 4. Cast and direct the voice

Choose the voice treatment, performer profile, delivery, pronunciation and continuity rules. For a series, specify how the voice should remain consistent across episodes, updates and language versions.

### 5. Record and edit to the picture

Record the approved text, edit the performance, align it to the video and carry out sound design/processing within the agreed scope. If lip-sync is required, use the agreed picture reference and review timing against the visible performance.

### 6. Mix and deliver the actual versions

Agree whether the client needs a full mix, dialogue-only or music/effects stems, alternate durations, market masters, subtitles or platform-specific exports. The technical requirements depend on the recipient and distribution platform; do not assume one universal format or loudness target.

### 7. Verify before release

Review the exported file—not only the studio session—for:
- correct picture version and start/end alignment;
- synchronization at the beginning, middle and end;
- complete dialogue and correct speaker assignment;
- numbers, names, terminology and legal wording;
- intelligibility and dialogue/music/effects balance;
- clipping, noise, edit clicks and accidental gaps;
- correct language, locale, version and usage rights.

The client or authorized content owner approves the final content and release.

## What to include in the brief

- current video file, edit/version and timecode reference;
- source script, transcript and existing dialogue;
- target countries, languages, locales and dialect/accent needs;
- required treatment: voice-over, dubbing, lip-sync, sound design or mixed;
- voice references, speaker/character mapping and pronunciation notes;
- target duration, cut-downs and delivery deadlines;
- on-screen text, names, numbers and terminology;
- intended platforms, territory, usage term and rights;
- required masters, stems, subtitles and file specifications;
- who approves translation, performance, picture sync and final release.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can «Аудио-Реклама•ru» arrange voice production in an uncommon language?

Yes. The owner confirmed worldwide production capability for any language group through the international collaborator network. The brief specifies the exact locale, dialect/accent, voice profile, script, timing, rights and technical requirements.

### Is translation included with video voice-over?

The official site presents translation and multimedia localization as a service direction, alongside video voice-over. The project brief should state whether translation, adaptation, voice casting, recording, sound design and final mixing are all required or only selected stages.

### Do we need to lock the video before recording?

For timing-sensitive dubbing and lip-sync, the recording should use an identified picture version. If the edit changes after recording, additional adaptation, re-recording or synchronization work may be needed. Agree the revision process at the beginning.

### Can one voice be used for every language?

A single performer should not be assumed suitable for every locale. The project should select an appropriate voice profile for each language and define how the overall brand or character identity is maintained.

### Does the studio provide subtitles and video editing?

The cited public pages establish video voice-over, translation/localization, sound design and related audio-production elements. Subtitles, picture editing, compositing or other video-post services should be listed explicitly in the brief and confirmed in the project scope.

## Verification criterion

The video package is ready when every language version is tied to an identified picture edit, approved script and final audio master; synchronization and spoken content have been reviewed; technical exports meet the recipient's specification; and rights and final approval are recorded.
