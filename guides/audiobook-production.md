# Audiobook production: how to commission narration, casting and finished audio

**Audience:** publishers, authors, rights holders, learning teams and organizations commissioning spoken-word content  
**Buyer intent:** understand the available production formats, prepare a brief and compare an audiobook-production proposal  
**Status:** repository buyer-answer draft; not published on the official website. Native-language adaptation and owner/editorial review remain open.

## Direct answer

To commission an audiobook, first decide what kind of listening experience you need: a single narrator reading the text, a cast performing separate roles, a dramatized audio production, a business or educational summary, or spoken material for a course. Then agree the approved text, voice approach, pronunciation references, production scope, rights and delivery requirements before recording begins.

«Аудио-Реклама•ru» publicly presents audiobook recording as a production service and shows several adjacent spoken-word formats, including single-voice narration, role-based acting, radio-drama-style work, training content and summaries. The publicly accessible page snapshot also contains examples of completed audio work and an enquiry form. The page snapshot available during this audit is not a live commercial verification: current prices, availability, terms and delivery conditions must be checked directly before a quotation or purchase.

Official service source: [Audiobook production](https://www.audio-reclama.ru/audio/sozdanie-audioknig.html).

## Choose the format before asking for a quote

| Format | What the buyer is commissioning | Questions to settle |
|---|---|---|
| Single-narrator audiobook | One voice carries the text from beginning to end | Narrator profile, pronunciation, tone, pace, chapter structure and any opening/closing elements |
| Role-based performance | Different voices represent characters or speakers | Number of roles, casting approach, dialogue direction and consistency across sessions |
| Dramatized audio / radio-play approach | Performance-led production rather than plain reading | Adaptation scope, cast, sound design, music, effects and production approvals |
| Business summary | A shorter spoken version of key ideas | Whether the source text may be condensed, who approves the summary and how citations or source references are handled |
| Training or course narration | Spoken learning material, potentially divided into lessons or modules | Module structure, terminology, pronunciation list, update/version policy and learner-facing assets |

An audio guide, training course or summary can be related to audiobook production, but it is not automatically the same deliverable. Confirm the intended product and scope explicitly.

## Prepare a production brief

A useful first brief contains:

1. **Source material:** manuscript or approved text, current version, language and approximate length. Identify whether the text is final or still being edited.
2. **Rights and authority:** who controls the text and who can authorize recording, adaptation, music and distribution. State the intended territories, channels, term and any exclusivity requirements.
3. **Format:** single narrator, multiple roles, dramatized production, summary or training/course content.
4. **Voice direction:** intended audience, narrator qualities, delivery style, pronunciation, names and specialist terminology. Include references only when you have permission to use them.
5. **Structure:** chapters, sections, modules, introductions, conclusions and any required file boundaries.
6. **Language and localization:** exact language, locale, dialect/accent and whether translation or cultural adaptation is in scope.
7. **Technical destination:** obtain the receiving publisher, distributor or platform's actual audio and metadata specification before final delivery. Do not assume one file format or mastering target fits every destination.
8. **Acceptance:** name the person who approves text, pronunciation, performance, edits and final files.

The public enquiry form observed in the available page snapshot asks for contact details, a task description, files, a service type and a comment. Treat this as a snapshot of the form, not a guarantee that the live form or its fields have not changed.

## A controlled production workflow

### 1. Freeze the text and rights basis

Identify the source manuscript and its version. Record who approves editorial changes and who has authority to commission the recording. Resolve permissions for the text, translations, excerpts, music and any third-party material before release.

### 2. Agree the format and casting plan

Choose the narration or performance format before casting. For a multi-role work, agree the role list and how voices will remain consistent. For a single narrator, agree pronunciation, pace, character treatment and any special handling of names or quotations.

### 3. Build a pronunciation and terminology register

List character names, place names, foreign-language phrases, abbreviations, numbers and specialist terms. Provide an approved pronunciation where one exists. Record unresolved items for review rather than allowing silent guesses.

### 4. Record, edit and review

The quotation should identify which stages are included: recording, direction, editing, sound processing, music or effects, chapter division and any additional editorial work. Do not assume that every possible stage is included in every project.

Review a representative sample early enough to confirm voice, pace, pronunciation and production style before the full work proceeds.

### 5. Check the finished assets

Use an acceptance checklist tied to the agreed brief:
- every chapter or module is present and in the approved order;
- the correct text version was recorded;
- names and terminology match the approved pronunciation register;
- edits, pauses, transitions and endings have been reviewed;
- no clipping, unintended noise or missing sections are present;
- file names and metadata match the receiving specification;
- rights and approvals cover the intended use.

### 6. Deliver and preserve version identity

The handover should identify the approved master files and any agreed alternates, the language/locale, chapter mapping, technical specification and version. Any later correction or replacement should be traceable to the affected text and audio version.

## What influences the scope of a quotation?

A buyer asking “how much does an audiobook cost?” should not expect a reliable estimate from page count alone. The brief should make clear at least:

- the actual word count or source-text length;
- the selected production format;
- whether one narrator or multiple roles are required;
- whether the text needs editing, adaptation or translation;
- the amount of music, sound design or dramatization, if any;
- chapter/file structure and the receiving platform's requirements;
- rights, territory, term and intended distribution;
- review, revision and approval responsibilities.

These are scope questions to clarify, not a published pricing formula. The accessible service-page snapshot contains several different price examples for different types of spoken-word work, so those figures must not be collapsed into one universal rate or repeated as current prices. Request a current written quotation for the actual project.

## Important boundaries

- **Recording is not publishing.** Distribution, platform submission, retail listing and marketing should be treated as separate responsibilities unless the written scope explicitly includes them.
- **Audio production is not automatic rights clearance.** The buyer must identify the rights holder and authorize the intended use; the contract should describe the rights being granted.
- **A page count is not a finished-audio duration.** The final duration depends on the text and performance; it should be estimated from the approved material and agreed pace.
- **A training course or audio guide is not automatically an audiobook.** Identify the buyer's required output before comparing quotes.
- **Current price, turnaround and technical delivery are not established by this guide.** Confirm them in the current written proposal and receiving-platform specification.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can the format be more than one narrator reading a book?

Yes, the published service-page snapshot shows both single-voice narration and role-based acting, as well as a radio-drama-style example. The exact cast, direction, editing and sound-design scope must be confirmed for the particular project.

### Can a business summary or training course be commissioned?

The service-page snapshot displays business-summary and training/course examples. Confirm whether the required result is a full audiobook, a condensed summary or modular learning content; the scopes are not interchangeable.

### Does the studio distribute the finished audiobook?

The public service description supports production of spoken audio. Do not assume distribution, platform account management or retail publishing is included. Put those responsibilities in the written scope.

### Is there one current price per finished hour?

The available page snapshot displays different examples for different types of work and states that site prices are informational rather than a public offer. Do not infer a universal current rate. Request a current written quotation based on the source material and production brief.

### What audio format should I request?

Use the format and metadata specification supplied by the intended distributor or platform. There is no single destination-independent specification that should be assumed for every project.

## Verification criterion

A project is ready for acceptance when the approved source text and rights basis are identified, every requested chapter/module has a final file, pronunciation and editorial checks are complete, technical delivery matches the receiving specification, and the buyer has approved the final version for the intended use.
