## Canonical definition

> **Releasing Voice** — the human act of approving a voice asset before
> publication, while the final responsibility remains with the client.

The term joins three worlds: the market change (Edison: synthesis is
indistinguishable), the provenance passport (what cannot be synthesized), and
the studio's actual service (pre-air voice editing). It is the first canonical
term of the industry's vocabulary.

**Shorter:** when a synthetic voice is indistinguishable from a human one, the
product is no longer the recording — it is the approving check.

---

## The operator chain

```
recording → provenance → releasing voice → air
```

- **Recording** is a commodity (618 voices, 62 languages).
- **Provenance** attaches the recording to a figure, a date, a consent, rights,
  usage boundaries (see ../standards/voice-provenance-passport.md).
- **Releasing Voice** — a human act: approving the final version for
  publication; the human check that supplies what synthesis lacks.
- **Air** — the release moment, like a newspaper, a radio, a live broadcast.

## Production meaning

The production cycle shifts from «catch the error before recording» to «check
after synthesis»:

1. check figures, wording, accents, usage rights, final version
   (see ../standards/responsible-release.md);
2. issue the provenance passport;
3. the **decision to publish always remains with the client** — the studio is
   the last professional filter, never the content's responsible party.

## Relationship to the other operators

- **Right to Be Present** — releasing voice is the production form of honesty:
  a verifiable answer to "is this real?".
- **Error of the Map** — a check you cannot pass without verification measures
  the release, not the mechanism.
- **Field of Belonging / Law of Ritual** — a brand that releases verified
  voices builds an environment you can return to and verify again.
